The fractional De Giorgi conjecture in dimension four for 1/2 < s < 1

October 7, 2026124 pagesAI-assisted candidate proof
Read the full PDFView source version →

A candidate proof of one-dimensional symmetry in dimension four for 1/2 < s < 1, beginning with the classification of bounded stable entire classical solutions in dimension three.

Candidate proofs. Verification ongoing. Both manuscripts state that verification of all proofs is continuing. This site summarizes the claims in the drafts; it does not establish their correctness.

Four-dimensional statement

Theorem 1.1, on page 2, states that for every 1/2 < s < 1, every bounded entire classical solution of (−Δ)su = u − u³ in ℝ⁴ with ∂₄u > 0 has the form

u(x) = qs(e · x − a)

e ∈ S³, e₄ > 0, a ∈ ℝ

No directional limits are assumed. The pointwise limits −1 and +1 in the increasing direction are part of the stated conclusion, rather than hypotheses. Uniform convergence in the transverse variables is not asserted.

The increasing layer qs is normalized by qs(0) = 0 and qs(t) → ±1 as t → ±∞. The fractional Laplacian uses the Fourier symbol |ξ|2s.

Stable solutions in dimension three

Theorem 1.2, on page 2, states that every bounded stable entire classical solution of the fractional Allen–Cahn equation in ℝ³, for 1/2 < s < 1, is either a constant well ±1 or a planar layer qs(e · x − a), with e ∈ S² and a ∈ ℝ.

Stability means nonnegativity of the second variation for compactly supported perturbations. This three-dimensional classification is the intermediate statement used to identify directional limits and establish global minimality for the monotone four-dimensional solution.

Route through the draft

  1. Actual transition sheets. Sections 4–7 construct local graphs, derive separation estimates, control layer profiles, and project the equation.
  2. Curvature and surface stability. Sections 8–12 develop curvature cancellation, an exact tangential trace operator, retained curvature under rescaling, and surface stability.
  3. Removal of exceptional zeros. Sections 13–14 use defect bounds and a capacity argument to close the stable three-dimensional classification.
  4. The four-dimensional conclusion. Sections 15–16 classify directional limits, establish global minimality, and apply minimizer rigidity to obtain the stated layer form.

The manuscript presents this chain of arguments as a candidate proof. Verification of all proofs is ongoing.
